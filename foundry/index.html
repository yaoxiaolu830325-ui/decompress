  1. Take orders from the book. Each carries a quantity, a unit price and a hard deadline. Reputation opens better clients.
  2. Materials are cash, up front. You pay the supplier when you accept. Deliver to collect the full order value.
  3. The slowest department sets the pace. Orders queue on the same machines, so a second order waits behind the first. The queue projection on every job tells you the truth.
  4. Deadlines are real. Ship late and the order is lost: salvage prices, a reputation hit, no payment. The shop saves itself, and runs a reduced night shift for up to 4 hours while you are away.

Works rules

The ledger, explained.

  1. Capacity. Each station delivers one station-second per second. A gate valve needs 8s machining, 5s assembly, 4s testing; ball valves and regulators need more. Your line rate is the slowest of the three.
  2. Cash & materials. Accepting an order reserves its materials immediately. Delivery pays the order value. Spare capacity feeds the spot market at auction prices.
  3. Reputation. On-time work earns 2–5 points. Missed deadlines cost 6; abandoned orders cost 8. Better reputation means better prices and bigger clients.
  4. Demand. Spot prices drift with each product's cycle and occasional demand spikes. Contract prices are locked when the offer appears.
  5. Saving. Autosaves every few seconds and when you leave. The night shift runs at 35% pace for up to 4 hours; deadlines pause while you are away.

Reset erases this browser's save permanently. The game is a work of fiction; all clients and companies are invented.
